Tailored abstract

When the Evidence Fades: Open-World Epistemic Coordination for Autonomous Observatory Swarms

Multi-agent systems research has mature tools for allocating tasks whose value is known in advance: auctions, consensus and distributed constraint optimisation. We argue that a growing class of autonomous systems faces a different problem. Agents must jointly decide which measurement to take next in order to learn which explanation of a phenomenon is true. The measurements that could separate explanations lose value irreversibly, and the true explanation may lie outside every agent's model. Satellite constellations following up fading astronomical transients are a sharp instance. Kilonovae, shock-cooling supernovae, afterglows and stellar flares look alike for hours, the discriminating observation exists only briefly, and spacecraft hold stale, partial beliefs between intermittent links. We frame this as open-world, deadline-aware epistemic coordination and sketch a concrete agent design: an explicit “none of the above” hypothesis, a utility that combines discrimination, model-adequacy evidence and opportunity decay, and a marginal-gain auction over stale beliefs. We then pose five open challenges for the agents community: valuing information under irreversibility, merging beliefs without double counting, coordinating on hypotheses rather than tasks, deciding when to stop discriminating and preserve evidence, and benchmarking against ground truth that is deliberately withheld.

Draft OpenReview statements

Why Blue Sky rather than the main track

“This paper defines a coordination problem, epistemic allocation under irreversible information decay with an open hypothesis set, that existing agent frameworks do not address. It proposes a design and a research agenda rather than an evaluated algorithm. A full empirical evaluation is preregistered and in progress, and we believe the problem framing and open challenges are of value to the community before those results exist.”
